At the highest levels of DJ competition, technical reliability is part of the performance environment.

Every turntable, mixer, monitoring system and audio connection must be prepared to deliver consistent operation under demanding live conditions.

Event Tech Research works with DMC to support the technical systems behind these performances, bringing together specialist DJ engineering, audio infrastructure, recording workflows and practical production experience.

The focus is on a stable, predictable technical environment in which competitors can concentrate on their performances. The performance itself stays with the DJs.

  • Rapid transitionsQuick changeovers between performers.
  • Specialist mixersBattle-style mixer configurations.
  • Precise vinyl controlFine mechanical tolerances at the turntable.
  • Consistent monitoringThe same conditions for each competitor.
  • Time-pressured reliabilitySystems that behave the same every set.

Engineered for the performance

From the competitor's equipment to the audience and the recording system. Select a stage to see what it does.

  • Turntables, cartridges and other DJ sources at each competition position. Set-up, testing and tested spares are prepared before the performance.

  • The performer's mixer, configured to the competition's requirements. Channel set-up and output levels are checked during soundcheck.

  • Splits and isolates the mixer outputs so the venue system and the recording path can each be fed independently. Analogue, digital or networked, depending on the venue.

  • The venue's PA and its engineers. The house team may remain responsible for mixing the room; the DJ feed is handed over at an agreed point.

  • What the room hears. The purpose of everything upstream is a consistent, predictable feed so the performance is what people notice.

  • Monitoring taken from the mixer or from venue routing so each performer hears what they need on stage. The source depends on the venue.

  • Independent capture of DJ sources and relevant microphones, plus a stereo reference. Channel identification and file management are part of the workflow.

The engineering behind the experience

Specialist engineering often stays invisible to the audience. It provides the technical environment the performance depends on.

Precision

DJ equipment preparation, turntable set-up, cartridge configuration and consistent operation from one position to the next.

Reliability

Signal integrity, tested equipment, spare systems, fault isolation and robust routing. The aim is to reduce risk and recover quickly. It does not promise uninterrupted operation.

Capture

Recording and preservation of individual performance audio sources for authorised post-event uses.

Evolution

Practical event-engineering experience informs future workflows, research and technical development.

No technical system is perfect, and none guarantees an outcome. The work reduces avoidable risk, makes faults easier to find and keeps recovery options ready. The performances remain the DJs'.

Development programme

Designing a competition-grade turntable isolation platform

The target is not simply a softer turntable support. Event Tech Research is developing a repeatable vibration-isolation platform that reduces structure-borne energy while remaining planted enough for aggressive scratch and turntablism techniques.

V1 prototype architecture

Rigid mass → controlled compliance → rigid mass

The current development direction retains the standard Technics SL-1210MK7 feet and places the deck on a rigid upper plate. A four-point Sorbothane isolation stage then separates that moving mass from a second rigid plate and the DJ booth.

The rigid plates are being treated as structural masses and load-distribution surfaces rather than as the vibration absorber themselves. The compliant element is deliberately concentrated in a measurable, replaceable pad stage.

Stability first. A design that produces impressive isolation figures but moves excessively during scratching is a failed competition design. The platform must reduce vibration without compromising cueing, backspins, fader work or forceful platter control.

Development tools

Building the measurement toolkit around the platform

Repeatability matters as much as the materials. Alongside the plates, the development programme uses simple mechanical measurement tools first, followed by instrumented vibration testing once the geometry is stable.

Digital / vernier caliper

Measures isolator thickness and cut dimensions, checks pad-to-pad consistency and records permanent set or compression changes.

Dial indicator + stand

Measures loaded compression, creep, recovery and small vertical movements. It also helps quantify how much the platform moves under controlled forces.

Feeler gauges

Checks whether all four support pads are genuinely engaged. This is especially important when investigating load sharing or any later centre-pad experiment.

Digital level

Checks platter and platform level and distinguishes simple vertical movement from pitch or roll during setup and force testing.

Scale + known test masses

Confirms the real supported mass and allows controlled load/deflection measurements instead of relying only on nominal component weights.

Layout and cutting tools

Steel rule, engineer's square, fine marker, rigid straightedge, cutting mat and sharp blades create repeatable pad sizes and support positions.

Accelerometers + acquisition

Planned Phase 2 instrumentation will compare vibration at the booth, lower plate, upper plate and turntable so frequency response can be measured rather than inferred.

Controlled excitation

Sweeps, broadband excitation and impact testing will be used alongside real scratching and PA/subwoofer tests to expose resonances and settling behaviour.

01 · Static characterisation

Measure pad dimensions, compression, creep, load sharing, platform level and movement under known forces.

02 · Dynamic validation

Measure booth-to-platform-to-turntable transfer from approximately 5–200 Hz and identify vertical, pitch and roll behaviour.

03 · Competition validation

Test baby scratches, transforms, chirps, flares, backspins, hard cueing, booth impacts and high-output PA conditions.

From prototype boards to purpose-designed plates

The bamboo blocks are a fast, rigid and repeatable development platform. They allow the isolation geometry to be proven before committing to a manufactured product.

The longer-term aim is to use the measured results to design purpose-built upper and lower vibration plates with repeatable isolator locations, simple alignment, fast event deployment and a footprint suited to the SL-1210MK7.

What the production design needs to achieve

  • Meaningful attenuation of structure-borne vibration in the frequency range that matters on a live stage.
  • Low lateral movement and fast settling during aggressive DJ operation.
  • Repeatable geometry across multiple competition positions.
  • Quick setup without permanent modification to the turntable.
  • Replaceable isolation elements so different load/stability options can be tested.
  • A practical system that can be deployed, checked and levelled by a technical team under event time pressure.

This is active Event Tech Research development work. Dimensions and materials above describe the current prototype direction, not a finished commercial specification. The system is not presented as approved or adopted by DMC, and final geometry will be determined by physical testing.
